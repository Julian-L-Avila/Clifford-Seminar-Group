\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}

This seminar investigates the Kepler problem across four physical domains
unified under the invariant language of Clifford geometric algebras. The
progression spans classical central force dynamics in $\Cl(3,0)$, spinor
regularization, relativistic motion and gauge gravity in the spacetime algebra
$\Cl(1,3)$, and the relativistic quantum field description of the hydrogen
atom via the Dirac--Hestenes equation.

The colloquium operates as a working research seminar. Participants engage as
collaborating researchers responsible for analytical rigour, formal mathematical
derivations, and critical evaluation of physical foundations. The program
comprises 18 weekly sessions of 90 minutes. Weeks~1 through~3 establish the
algebraic foundation, while Weeks~4 through~18 are conducted by senior--junior
tandems.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Working Roles}
\label{subsec:roles}

The seminar avoids passive lecture formats. Each session relies on four distinct
responsibilities.

\begin{description}
  \item[Referat (Lead Exposition)]
  Delivered jointly by the assigned tandem. The presentation focuses on direct,
  blackboard derivations of primary equations. Conceptual assumptions and
  algebraic transitions take precedence over projected slides.
  \item[Korreferat (Respondent Critique)]
  An assigned participant presents a critical commentary immediately following
  the Referat. The respondent examines implicit hypotheses, compares the results
  with traditional coordinate or matrix formalisms, identifies potential
  pathologies, and formulates targeted questions for discussion.
  \item[Thesenpapier (Position Paper)]
  A concise memorandum circulated by the presenting tandem at least five days
  prior to the session. It contains formal definitions, key algebraic identity
  statements, and explicit theses concerning physical interpretation.
  Participants study this document in advance.
  \item[Sitzungsprotokoll (Session Record)]
  A rotating participant documents the derivation steps, the resolution of
  theoretical debates, and open problems. The finalized protocol is archived in
  the shared repository within seven days following the session.
\end{description}

\subsection{Tandem Collaboration}
\label{subsec:tandems}

Weeks~4 through~18 pair an advanced participant (senior) with an earlier-stage
student (junior). The senior ensures mathematical coherence and contextualizes
advanced algebraic techniques. The junior works directly through the derivation
machinery, receiving structured guidance in multilinear structures and
relativistic dynamics. Responsibilities for blackboard presentation are shared
equally between both speakers.

\subsection{Session Structure}
\label{subsec:structure}

Preparatory meetings with the seminar director occur two weeks and five days
prior to each session. Every attendee must submit at least one written question
on the Thesenpapier 24 hours before meeting. The 90-minute session adheres to the
schedule in \cref{tab:session_structure}.
